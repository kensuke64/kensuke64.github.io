  1. The Daughter Effect in Politics: Evidence from Japanese National Legislators

    with Taketo Hara, Ritsu Kitagawa, and Takeru Sone. March 2026.

    Working paper↗

    Abstract

    We examine how politicians’ family structure shapes policy preferences, focusing on the influence of daughters on attitudes toward gender-and family-related policies. Using original data on the number and gender of children of candidates elected to Japan’s House of Representatives in 2021, combined with candidate survey responses collected during the election campaign, we analyze whether exposure to daughters affects positions on optional separate surnames for married couples, same-sex marriage, and legislation promoting understanding of sexual minorities. Exploiting plausibly exogenous variation in the number of daughters conditional on the number of children, we find that daughters are associated with more progressive policy positions among male candidates. Our findings highlight the role of family-based socialization in shaping elite political preferences and extend evidence on the “daughter effect” to a non-Western context.
