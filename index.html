About

I am a first-year master’s student in economics at Waseda University. Before starting my master’s, I obtained a B.A. in Social Sciences from Waseda University, with a year on exchange at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

My research interests lie in personnel economics and organizational economics. I study how managers, information, and AI-based technologies shape workers’ careers and organizational outcomes inside firms.

In joint work with Guido Friebel, Hideo Owan, and Ruo Shangguan, I study how private information that supervisors accumulate about their workers continues to shape careers long after the supervisory relationship ends. Using personnel records from a large Japanese manufacturing firm, we ask whether such dormant soft information is reactivated when a former direct supervisor is promoted into a senior position, and trace how the rising standing of past information holders feeds back into subordinates’ own promotion prospects within the firm’s internal labor market. I co-organize the Personnel Economics Workshop, a monthly seminar series held on the fourth Saturday of each month at Waseda University.
